# The phone line starts as a keypad IVR on Exotel; conversational voice follows on LiveKit

Many Students and parents have feature phones, patchy data and no app. A keypad menu (press 1 for applications, 2 for payments) works on every phone, needs no speech recognition, and can be built and tested now; conversational voice over LiveKit (ADR 0010) needs a SIP trunk and a speech provider for each language, which we do not have yet. So the phone line starts as an IVR: Exotel calls our webhook on each keypress, and our state machine answers with the text to speak and whether to wait for a key.

The IVR identifies the caller by their registered mobile number and speaks the same grounded answers JAGO gives in chat, built by the same code from the Student's own records, so the two can never disagree. It says nothing about a case to an unregistered number. A caller who wants a person leaves a callback request, which reaches their district office's queue for approval like any other Nudge. The engine is provider-neutral: Exotel's dynamic Gather format is one adapter, and a browser phone in the simulators drives the same engine for development and demos. When LiveKit and speech models are in place, the menu becomes the fallback for callers who prefer keys.
